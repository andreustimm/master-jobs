# ADR-002: Target Tracks as the Unit of Fit

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

The fit score is a weighted rubric computed against one matching profile per
candidate. Andreus's profile targets AI and architecture roles; he prefers those
but accepts Laravel, PHP, TypeScript, React, Java or Tech Lead roles to get work
sooner — a need common among candidates. Under the current profile a remote
"Senior Laravel Developer" lands around 34–41 (manual estimate): `php` and
`laravel` weigh 2 each out of 287 keyword points and no target title is a PHP
title. The Jobs screen hides anything under 45 by default, so even an ingested
Laravel job stays invisible. Persona P3 (Renata, in career transition) has the
same shape of need: a desired trajectory her current profile does not describe.

The profile already allows several title clusters, but keywords (including
negatives such as `wordpress` at −6), compensation floor and seniority are one
per profile, and the profile is one per candidate. A typical Latin America PHP
salary (USD 3–6k/month) sits under the USD 7.5k/month floor and zeroes the pay
component. Market products (LinkedIn "Open to Work" with up to 5 target titles,
Teal, Jobscan) let a candidate hold several targets, but they filter by target
and keep a single score; none scores per target.

## Decision

Introduce **target tracks**. Every candidate has exactly one **primary** track
and may add **accepted** tracks. Each track owns its target titles (with
optional title clusters), weighted keywords including negatives, target
seniority and compensation ranges — the structure the profile already uses:
independent ranges per currency and period, each with floor, target and ideal,
never converted from one another (M-01). Work eligibility, blockers, work mode, years of
experience and evidence stay person-level and apply to every track. Scoring per
target, rather than filtering one score by target, is deliberate: the rubric
must explain why a job fits the target it is shown under. Every job is
scored against each active track; the Jobs screen opens on the primary track and
offers a selector for another track or "all tracks", where each job appears once
with the fit and label of the track it fits best.

A new track is **suggested, then confirmed**: from a name and a term, the system
pre-fills titles and keywords from the skills catalog and the candidate's
evidence; nothing reaches the score until the candidate confirms. The suggestion
is deterministic — no LLM on the scoring path.

The candidate's own matching profile becomes the primary track. A candidate
without an own profile has a pending primary track and no ranking: the system
default profile never scores another candidate (M-06). Evidence used by
suggestions and gap markers is only the candidate's own.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep one profile and let term results bypass the fit cut

- **Description**: Show jobs found by a term ranked by term relevance, with
  their (low) fit visible but not filtering.
- **Pros**: No new concept; minimal scoring change.
- **Cons**: The main board stays blind to accepted roles; a Laravel job is
  still judged against "Staff AI Architect", so the number shown is misleading.
- **Why rejected**: A fit that measures the wrong target is not auditable.

### Alternative 2: Widen the single profile

- **Description**: Add PHP/Laravel titles and keywords to the one profile.
- **Pros**: Fastest change.
- **Cons**: Mixes two rankings; PHP roles compete with AI roles for the top and
  dilute the primary positioning.
- **Why rejected**: The candidate explicitly prefers AI; the primary ranking
  must not be invaded.

### Alternative 3: Everything per track, including eligibility

- **Description**: Let a track override geography and work mode.
- **Pros**: Maximum flexibility.
- **Cons**: A track could contradict a fact about the person ("no US work
  authorization" is not a preference).
- **Why rejected**: Eligibility is a fact, not a target.

### Alternative 4: Mix all tracks by default, or discount accepted tracks

- **Description**: Open the board on "all tracks", optionally multiplying the
  fit of accepted tracks by a factor so the primary wins close ties.
- **Pros**: Everything visible at once.
- **Cons**: Accepted roles can dominate the top by default; a discounted number
  is no longer the rubric's fit.
- **Why rejected**: The primary ranking opens by default; the number shown is
  always a pure fit.

## Consequences

### Positive

- A job is measured against the target it actually fits; the rubric stays
  explainable component by component.
- The primary ranking is untouched by default.
- Serves career transition (P3) without special cases.

### Negative

- Scoring cost grows with the number of active tracks.
- One more concept in the candidate's mental model.

### Risks

- Changing the scorer requires bumping `SCORER_VERSION` and a full rescore
  (AGENTS.md rule 6); two score generations must never coexist silently.
- Fit per track changes the identity of a fit (today one per candidate and job,
  ADR 0007's fit assessment); the migration follows the safe-migration process.
- Per-track fits multiply stored score rows; production storage is limited, so
  enabling tracks in production needs a storage estimate within budget.

## Implementation Notes

Per-track floor and seniority feed the existing comp and seniority components;
blockers and geography are evaluated once per job.
