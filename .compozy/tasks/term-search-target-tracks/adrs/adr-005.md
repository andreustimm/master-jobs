# ADR-005: The Jobs Screen Is the Only Results Surface

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

The candidate's stated flow is: "I want more jobs about php, Laravel, techlead
or java; the system fetches them for me; on the Jobs screen I filter." Today the
Jobs screen matches free text only against title and company (a `LIKE`), never
the description, so `Laravel` — which usually appears in the description — is
missed even for jobs already in the corpus. The screen also offers only a
"discloses pay" toggle and a "sort by pay" that compares raw numbers across
currencies and periods. The candidate asked for a pay-value filter as a plain
filter on this screen.

## Decision

Jobs brought in by saved terms are browsed on the existing **Jobs screen**,
which gains:

- **Track selector** (ADR-002): opens on the primary track.
- **Term filter over the corpus**: matches title, company and description,
  whole-word with the scorer's term boundary, with the term normalization of
  ADR-004. It works for any typed term, saved or not, and returns what is
  already in the corpus. It replaces today's substring match on title and
  company: `java` no longer finds `JavaScript`, and `Lara` no longer finds
  `Laravel`. Proximity or semantic matching from
  `platform-catalog-hybrid-search` may reorder results or show a separately
  labeled group, never change what this filter returns.
- **"Brought by my term" filter**: narrows to jobs a given saved term brought in.
- **Minimum pay filter**: an amount, a currency and a period. Only a filter — it
  never goes to platforms, is not part of a saved term and does not change the
  fit. Amounts are compared after converting currency with stored exchange
  rates and normalizing the period with the scorer's conventions (2,080 hours a
  year; project pay with duration annualized). The conversion serves only this
  filter; the candidate's compensation ranges stay independent per currency
  (M-01). A job qualifies when the top of its range reaches the minimum. Jobs that disclose no pay, or whose pay cannot be
  compared, stay visible, marked, after the qualifying ones; a toggle hides
  them. Jobs whose disclosed pay is below the minimum are hidden.
- **Sort by pay** uses the same normalized amount.

When a term filter returns few or no results, the screen offers to save that
term as a saved term search, pre-filled.

There is no separate results page for saved terms.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: A dedicated results page per saved term

- **Description**: Each saved term has its own list.
- **Pros**: Clear provenance.
- **Cons**: Two places to triage jobs; filters duplicated; the candidate asked
  to filter on the Jobs screen.
- **Why rejected**: It splits the decision surface — and the product's
  bottleneck is the decision, not discovery.

### Alternative 2: Wait for the hybrid-search PRD to cover descriptions

- **Description**: Leave corpus search to `platform-catalog-hybrid-search`.
- **Pros**: No overlap between PRDs.
- **Cons**: That PRD is planned, not sequenced; the candidate's first failed
  search stays failed.
- **Why rejected**: Whole-word matching is the same rule the scorer already
  uses; the hybrid PRD can later improve ordering on top of it.

### Alternative 3: Hide jobs without disclosed pay under a minimum-pay filter

- **Description**: "At least X" shows only jobs that disclose at least X.
- **Pros**: Short, precise list.
- **Cons**: Most remote postings do not disclose pay; the filter would hide
  most good jobs. AGENTS.md rule 8: missing data is neutral, never punitive.
- **Why rejected**: Absence of data is not evidence of low pay.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One triage surface; filters live in the URL like the existing ones.
- The candidate's original failed search (`Laravel` in the corpus) now works.

### Negative

- The Jobs screen gains three controls; mobile layout must stay usable.
- The search field changes meaning (whole-word, with descriptions); partial
  company names no longer match, and living QA scenarios for it reset.

### Risks

- Description matching over a large corpus may be slow; performance targets are
  stated in the PRD and the mechanism is a TechSpec decision.

## Implementation Notes

Currency conversion reuses the stored FX rates and the money value object.
