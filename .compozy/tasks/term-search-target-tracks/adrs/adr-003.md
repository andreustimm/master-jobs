# ADR-003: Every Saved Term Belongs to Exactly One Track

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-18

## Context

Target tracks (ADR-002) decide which target a job is measured against. A saved
term such as `java` is the candidate's statement of what kind of job they want
more of. If the term had no track, the jobs it brings in would be scored only
against existing tracks — a Java job measured against an AI track falls back
under the fit cut, reproducing the original problem.

## Decision

Every saved term belongs to exactly one track. When saving a term, the
candidate either links it to an existing track (`techlead` → AI/Architecture;
`php` and `Laravel` → PHP/Laravel) or creates a new track pre-filled from the
term, which the candidate confirms (ADR-002). A track may hold several terms.
Moving a term to another track is allowed; archiving a track pauses its terms.

Jobs a term brings in are still scored against every active track — the link
guarantees a fitting target exists, not that the job is scored only there.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Free-standing terms with an optional track

- **Description**: A term may exist without a track; its jobs are scored
  against all tracks and keep the best fit.
- **Pros**: One fewer step when saving.
- **Cons**: `java` without a Java track produces Java jobs under the cut again;
  the candidate asks for jobs and does not see them.
- **Why rejected**: It silently recreates the failure the feature exists to fix.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The fit of jobs a term brings in is measured against a target that fits them.
- The terms screen reads as "what I want, grouped by what I aim for".

### Negative

- Saving a term for a new area also asks for a track confirmation.

### Risks

- Candidates may create near-duplicate tracks; mitigated by suggesting an
  existing track when the term already appears in one of its keywords.

## Implementation Notes

The suggestion of an existing track uses the same term normalization as the
search (ADR-004).
