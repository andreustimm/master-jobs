# ADR-001: Candidates grant access by the recruiter's email, with an invitation for people without an account

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-06

## Context

Rule G25 says only the candidate creates a link with a recruiter, and a new
account never points at an existing candidate. The `recruiter_candidate` table
and the `linkCandidate`/`unlinkById` functions exist, but no candidate screen or
action uses them: today links are created only by tests, and recruiters signing
up through #464 land on an empty state that already tells them the candidate
grants access. The candidate needs a way to name the recruiter. The recruiter
may already have an account or may not. Google Drive's sharing model (invite by
email, code for people without an account, revoke in the same place) is the
reference the owner pointed to.

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-06.

## Decision

- The candidate grants access by typing the recruiter's email on their account
  page.
- If an enabled account with the recruiter role and that verified email
  exists, access starts immediately and the recruiter receives an email saying
  so.
- If no such account exists, the recruiter receives an invitation email with a
  single-use link that expires (ADR-006). The link leads to the single sign-up
  screen from #464 with the Recruiter role preselected. Access starts only
  when the account is confirmed with that same email, verified by the provider
  or by the emailed code. An invitation completed with a different email grants
  nothing.
- An existing account with that email but without the recruiter role is
  treated as "no recruiter account": the invitation is sent, and nothing is
  revealed to the candidate about the account (see Business Rules for the
  neutral outcome).

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Recruiter requests, candidate approves

- **Description**: The recruiter searches for a candidate and requests access;
  the candidate approves.
- **Pros**: Recruiter can start the relationship.
- **Cons**: Lets any self-signed-up recruiter probe which candidates exist;
  conflicts with G25 and with the 404-on-unknown policy.
- **Why rejected**: Leaks existence and moves initiative away from the data
  owner.

### Alternative 2: Shareable link without email

- **Description**: The candidate generates a link and sends it anywhere;
  whoever opens it gains access.
- **Pros**: No email delivery dependency.
- **Cons**: A forwarded link grants access to the wrong person; consent is not
  tied to an identified recipient (LGPD art. 8).
- **Why rejected**: Consent must name the recipient.

### Alternative 3: Accept an invitation with any email

- **Description**: The invitation link binds access to whoever completes it.
- **Pros**: Fewer failures when the recruiter uses another address.
- **Cons**: Same forwarding risk as Alternative 2.
- **Why rejected**: The candidate consented to a specific address.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Consent is tied to an identified, verified email.
- G25 holds: only the candidate starts a link, and sign-up alone never creates
  one.

### Negative

- A recruiter who wants to use another address needs a new invitation from
  the candidate.

### Risks

- The invitation flow becomes an email-sending surface for abuse; mitigated by
  the daily cap in ADR-006.
- Revealing whether an email has a recruiter account; mitigated by the neutral
  outcome described in the PRD.

## Implementation Notes

Reuse the #464 sign-up screen and the `Mailer` port with localized builders.
The access record keeps the email the candidate typed (normalized) so that the
later sign-up can be matched.
