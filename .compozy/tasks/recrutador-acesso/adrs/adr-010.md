# ADR-010: Remove the "Recruiters" profile visibility; access to recruiters is by invitation only

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-06

## Context

Candidate profiles have three visibilities (`VISIBILITIES` in
`src/contexts/auth/domain/types.ts`): Private, Recruiters and Public. The
option is offered on the candidate page (`app/candidate/page.tsx`), with labels
in the dictionary. `can()` (`src/contexts/auth/domain/policy.ts`) lets **any
authenticated recruiter** read a profile set to "Recruiters", with no grant.
That made sense when only administrators created recruiter accounts. Since
#464, anyone with a verified email can sign up as a recruiter. "Recruiters"
therefore now means "anyone who signs up", without consent per recipient,
without a record and without a way to revoke one person. That contradicts the
consent model of this PRD (ADR-001, ADR-004, ADR-005).

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-06.

## Decision

- Remove the "Recruiters" visibility. Only Private and Public remain.
- Profiles currently set to "Recruiters" become Private. This is a
  non-additive data migration and needs human review at promotion (ADR 0028).
- `can()` stops granting reads because of a `recruiters` visibility. A
  recruiter reads a candidate only through an active grant (this PRD), or
  through the public profile `/p/[slug]` when the candidate chose Public.
- The candidate page and the dictionary drop the option and its hint.
- A candidate who was on "Recruiters" sees a notice on the account page saying
  the profile is now Private and that recruiters get access by invitation. No mass
  email is sent.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep "Recruiters" but restrict it to granted recruiters

- **Description**: The option stays, and it means "recruiters I granted".
- **Pros**: No data migration, and the label stays familiar.
- **Cons**: It duplicates the grant: a granted recruiter already reads the
  candidate. The option would then do nothing or mean something unclear.
- **Why rejected**: A setting with no effect of its own confuses the candidate.

### Alternative 2: Keep it as is

- **Description**: Any authenticated recruiter reads "Recruiters" profiles.
- **Pros**: No change.
- **Cons**: With open recruiter sign-up, this is a near-public exposure that
  has no record and no per-person revocation. It bypasses the consent model.
- **Why rejected**: Incompatible with consent per recipient (LGPD art. 8).

## Consequences

### Positive

- One way for a recruiter to see a non-public profile: the candidate's grant.
- The policy loses a branch that depended on open recruiter sign-up being
  absent.

### Negative

- Recruiters who read "Recruiters" profiles without a link lose that view.
- Candidates who relied on the option must invite recruiters one by one.

### Risks

- The migration changes existing data. It is non-additive, waits for human
  review at promotion (ADR 0028) and is not reversed automatically. The list
  of affected candidates is recorded so that each one gets the notice.
- Stored values or links still carrying `recruiters` after the migration are
  treated as Private, never as Public.

## Implementation Notes

The visibility type, `can()`, the candidate page options, the dictionary keys
(`visibility.recruiters`, `visibility.recruitersHint`) and their tests change
together. The notice is shown on the candidate's account page, in the Recruiter
access section, until the candidate dismisses it.
