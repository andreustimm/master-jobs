# ADR-004: Sign-in methods coexist and the last method cannot be removed

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-05

## Context

Accounts can sign in with password or with a CLI-generated magic link. Social sign-in adds Google and LinkedIn, and self-sign-up (ADR-002) creates accounts that have no password at all. Removing methods carelessly can lock a person out.

## Decision

- Password, magic link, Google and LinkedIn coexist on the same account. Linking a provider never removes the password.
- An account may have only social methods (self-sign-up or invited account that never set a password). Such a person may add a password later from the account page.
- The account page lists every method with its link date and last use. Unlinking a provider is refused when it would leave the account with no way to sign in (no password and no other provider). Magic link is not counted as a method the person controls, since only the CLI issues it.
- Administrators can see and unlink another account's providers, under the same last-method protection, but can never link a provider to someone else's account. The CLI (`jho auth`) lists and unlinks providers with the same rules.
- A disabled account cannot sign in by any method; signing in socially never re-enables it.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Password always required

- **Description**: Every account keeps a password as fallback.
- **Pros**: Always a non-social way in.
- **Cons**: Self-sign-up would force password creation, adding friction.
- **Why rejected**: The owner chose coexistence with social-only accounts allowed.

### Alternative 2: Social replaces password

- **Description**: Linking a provider deletes the password.
- **Pros**: Single method to secure.
- **Cons**: Lock-out when the provider is unavailable.
- **Why rejected**: Unnecessary risk.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Nobody gets locked out by unlinking.

### Negative

- Social-only accounts depend on the provider account's availability.

### Risks

- If a social-only person loses the provider account, recovery depends on the password-recovery flow with a known email; documented in the PRD.

## Implementation Notes

Last-method checks run at the moment of unlinking, against the current state, so two concurrent unlinks cannot both succeed when only two methods remain.
