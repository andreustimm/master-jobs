# ADR-001: Identify social logins by provider subject; link existing accounts by verified email or from the account page

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-05

## Context

master-jobs accounts are created by an administrator (`/admin/users`, `jho auth add-user`) and identified by an email address the administrator typed. That email is never verified by the product (email confirmation depends on Resend, issue #237). The owner wants people to sign in with Google or LinkedIn, and — when an account with that email already exists and is not yet linked to the provider — to have the first social sign-in link it automatically.

Both providers state that the email claim must not be used as a unique identifier; the stable key is the OpenID Connect `sub`, which LinkedIn issues per application (pairwise). LinkedIn may omit `email` or `email_verified`. The known attack on social linking is account takeover through automatic linking on an unverified email. Security rule G25 forbids a new account from pointing at an existing candidate.

## Decision

- A social identity is keyed by **(provider, subject)**, never by email. After the first link, sign-in resolves the account by that key only, even if the email at the provider later changes.
- **Automatic link:** on a social sign-in whose (provider, subject) is unknown, if the provider asserts the email **as verified** and an account with the same normalized email exists, has no identity from that provider yet, and is enabled, the identity is linked to that account and the person is signed in.
- **Manual link:** a signed-in person can link Google or LinkedIn from the account page, regardless of email match. This covers different emails and providers that do not assert verification.
- One identity per provider per account; one account per (provider, subject).
- Every link and unlink is recorded as an audit event and listed on the account page.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Manual link only

- **Description**: Social sign-in never links by itself; the person first signs in with password or magic link and connects the provider from the account page.
- **Pros**: Eliminates takeover via email matching.
- **Cons**: Accounts created by the administrator have no password, so the first access needs a CLI-generated magic link; high friction.
- **Why rejected**: The owner explicitly wants existing accounts linked on first social sign-in.

### Alternative 2: Link by email even without provider verification

- **Description**: Match any email the provider returns.
- **Pros**: Works for every LinkedIn member.
- **Cons**: Classic pre-hijacking/takeover vector; violates the spirit of G25.
- **Why rejected**: Unsafe; the manual link covers the unverified case.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Invited people (accounts without password) can enter with one click.
- Email changes at the provider do not break or redirect sign-in.

### Negative

- LinkedIn members whose email is not asserted as verified must link manually after signing in another way.

### Risks

- An administrator typo in an account email could let the owner of the typed inbox take the account. Mitigation: the link is recorded and shown on the account page and to administrators, who can unlink providers and disable accounts.

## Implementation Notes

Email comparison uses the same normalization as account emails today. Disabled accounts never link or sign in.
